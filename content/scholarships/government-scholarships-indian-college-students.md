A college degree is expensive, but the government runs several scholarship schemes that can take a large part of the cost off your shoulders. Many students miss out simply because they never heard of the right scheme or missed the application window. Bookmark our [scholarships section](/scholarships/) for deadline alerts through the year.

Every scheme below is real and currently active, and applications for most central schemes go through the <a href="https://scholarships.gov.in" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Scholarship Portal (NSP)</a>. Eligibility rules and deadlines change every year, so always read the current guidelines on the NSP before you apply.

## PM-USP Central Sector Scheme for College and University Students

This is the best-known central scholarship for college students. Run by the Department of Higher Education, it supports students who performed well in Class 12 but need financial help for higher studies.

The scheme pays Rs. 12,000 per year for the first three years of an undergraduate course and Rs. 20,000 per year for postgraduate study. Engineering students get Rs. 20,000 in the fourth year. Around 82,000 fresh scholarships are awarded every year, and half are reserved for girls.

To be eligible, you need to be above the 80th percentile of successful candidates in your Class 12 board stream, enrolled in a regular degree course at a recognised institution, and your family's annual income must not exceed Rs. 4.5 lakh. Diploma students and distance-learning students are not eligible. Renewal each year requires at least 50% marks and 75% attendance.

## AICTE Pragati Scholarship for Girl Students

Girls studying in AICTE-approved technical institutions, both degree and diploma courses, can apply for the Pragati scheme. It pays Rs. 50,000 per year towards college fees, books, equipment, and other study expenses.

Students admitted to the first year, or to the second year through lateral entry, are eligible. The family's annual income should not exceed Rs. 8 lakh, and up to two girls per family can receive it. Selection is merit-based, using a state-wise merit list prepared from qualifying exam marks. Applications and renewals go through the NSP.

## AICTE Saksham Scholarship for Specially-Abled Students

Saksham is the parallel AICTE scheme for students with a benchmark disability of at least 40%. It also pays Rs. 50,000 per year for the duration of the technical course, with the same income limit of Rs. 8 lakh per year.

The application needs a disability certificate issued by the competent medical authority, an income certificate, marksheets, and admission proof. The amount is paid through Direct Benefit Transfer after the institute verifies the application.

## INSPIRE Scholarship for Higher Education

If you are studying basic or natural sciences, the INSPIRE-SHE programme of the Department of Science and Technology is worth looking at. It offers 12,000 scholarships every year, each valued at Rs. 80,000 per year.

One of the main eligibility routes is a top-1% rank in the Class 12 board examination. Covered subjects include physics, chemistry, mathematics, biology, and other approved basic and natural science subjects. The application process is announced on the INSPIRE programme's website, and students should apply when the cycle opens.

## Post-Matric Scholarships for SC, ST, OBC, and Minority Students

These are some of the largest scholarship programmes in the country, run by central ministries with implementation through the states. They support students from Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, Other Backward Classes, and minority communities studying at the college level.

The exact income limits and benefit amounts differ by category and state, and applications are usually submitted on the NSP or on the state's own scholarship portal. Because these are state-implemented, the list of documents and the income ceiling in your state may differ from your neighbour's, so check your state's social welfare or minority welfare department page.

## Merit-cum-Means Scholarship for Minority Students

Students from minority communities pursuing professional or technical courses at the undergraduate or postgraduate level can apply for the merit-cum-means scholarship. It is meant for students who have done well academically but cannot afford the course fees. Applications are filed on the NSP, and the family income limit and award amounts follow the scheme's current guidelines.

## How to apply without making mistakes

Start with a one-time registration on the National Scholarship Portal. You will need an Aadhaar number, and your bank account must be linked to Aadhaar, because the money reaches you only through DBT. Keep scanned copies of your marksheets, income certificate, caste or disability certificate, admission receipt, and a recent photograph ready before you start.

Apply before the deadline and track your application status on the portal. After you apply, your college verifies the application, and then the state nodal officer does a second check. If something is flagged as defective, you get a correction window, so respond quickly instead of waiting for the next year.

It is also worth checking your own state government's scholarship portal. States run their own schemes for college students, especially post-matric scholarships and merit awards, which sometimes have higher income limits or cover courses the central schemes do not. Your college's scholarship cell is usually the best place to learn which state schemes are open right now. And do not ignore private schemes — the [LIC Golden Jubilee Scholarship 2026](/scholarships/lic-golden-jubilee-scholarship-2026/) offered up to Rs 50,000 a year to first-year students.
