**The Karnataka Examinations Authority has declared the KSET 2026 provisional result, and 8,171 candidates have qualified for Assistant Professor eligibility across 37 subjects.** The result came out with a notification dated November 20, 2026, along with the subject-wise and category-wise cut-off marks. If you sat for the exam on October 11, your name could be on the list.

KSET is the test that decides who can apply for Assistant Professor posts in degree colleges and universities across Karnataka. Passing it does not give you a job by itself. It gives you the eligibility certificate you need when a college or university opens teaching posts. The [KSET 2026 result](/results/) is published as a PDF on the KEA portal, and it lists qualified candidates by application number.

## Why 8,171 qualified against 7,204 slots

The number looks odd at first. KEA had worked out 7,204 qualifying slots across all 37 subjects and categories, yet 8,171 names appear on the list. The reason is in the UGC rules KEA follows.

Under those rules, the total number of qualifying slots is fixed at 6% of the candidates who appeared for both papers. That figure is then split subject by subject and category by category, based on the marks candidates scored. But KEA has made it clear that every candidate who meets the prescribed cut-off for their subject and category must be included in the qualified list, even if the total goes past the slots. That is why the final count is higher.

The scale of the exam shows why the list is long. A total of 1,29,214 candidates had registered for KSET 2026, and 1,17,638 of them appeared for both Paper 1 and Paper 2. The exam was held in offline mode on October 11 across 11 districts of Karnataka.

> "All candidates who secure the prescribed cut-off marks in their subject and category must be included in the qualified list, even if the number exceeds the available slots."

## Cut-off marks: the big subjects, General Merit

KEA has published the cut-off for all 37 subjects. The figures below are the General Merit cut-offs out of 300 marks for some of the most popular subjects. Candidates from reserved categories, persons with disabilities and transgender candidates should check the detailed cut-off PDF for their own qualifying marks.

- Commerce: 182
- Management: 196
- Education: 196
- Mass Communication and Journalism: 190
- English: 176
- Political Science: 176
- Computer Science and Applications: 172
- Economics: 164
- History: 160
- Kannada: 158

Two conditions had to be met before a candidate could qualify. First, the minimum aggregate marks: 40% for General category candidates and 35% for reserved category candidates. Second, the subject-wise and category-wise cut-off for that candidate's own subject. Clearing the minimum alone was not enough. Both had to be cleared together.

## How to check your KSET 2026 result in five steps

The result is only on the official portal. Do not trust screenshots or links passed around in WhatsApp groups, because fake result pages appear within hours of every big result. Type the address yourself.

- Open the <a href="https://cetonline.karnataka.gov.in/kea" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official KEA website</a> and find the KSET 2026 result notification
- Open the subject-wise and category-wise result PDF for your subject
- Search for your application number in the list
- Check the separate cut-off PDF to confirm the qualifying marks for your category
- Download both PDFs and keep them saved for the verification stage

If your name is on the list, keep your documents ready. KEA has said the eligibility, self-declarations and supporting documents of qualified candidates will be verified as per the detailed KSET 2026 notification. The category and slot allocations were fixed on the basis of the claims you made at the time of application, and KEA has said changes in category or other particulars will not be allowed at this stage.

## How the result was prepared

KEA followed a three-stage process under UGC guidelines and the Karnataka reservation policy. First, the total qualifying slots were fixed at 6% of those who appeared for both papers. Next, those slots were divided subject-wise and category-wise on the basis of aggregate marks. Last, the minimum qualifying marks were applied, 40% for General and 35% for reserved categories.

Before the result, KEA had invited objections to the provisional answer key. Subject experts looked at the submissions, and the final answer key was then used to evaluate the answer sheets. Provisional marks were published on November 6, and the provisional merit list followed.

The result is provisional for now. KEA will issue eligibility certificates to qualified candidates shortly, though no date has been announced.
