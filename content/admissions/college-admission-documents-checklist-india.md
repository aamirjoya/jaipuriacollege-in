Getting into college is exciting, but the paperwork can catch you off guard. Every year, thousands of students rush between photocopy shops and gazetted officers on the last day of admission because one paper was missing. A little preparation saves you that stress.

The exact list varies from college to college, and from course to course. The list below covers what most colleges in India ask for. Read your own college's admission notice carefully, because the notice is the final word.

## Academic records: your marksheets and certificates

Your Class 10 and Class 12 marksheets sit at the top of every checklist. Colleges use them to check eligibility and, in merit-based admissions, to calculate cut-offs. Most colleges also ask for the passing certificates from both classes. The Class 10 certificate usually doubles as proof of your date of birth.

Carry originals plus two or three self-attested photocopies of each. If your board has not issued the original Class 12 marksheet yet, many colleges accept a downloaded copy from the board's website or a provisional marksheet for the first round. You will still need to submit the original when it arrives, so confirm the deadline with the college office.

## Transfer certificate, migration certificate, and character certificate

The transfer certificate (TC) is issued by the school or college you last attended. It states that you have left that institution and are free to join a new one. Most colleges will not complete admission without it, so request it early. School offices can take a few days.

The migration certificate is different. It is issued by your board or university and is usually needed when you move from one board to another, for example from a state board to a university that asks for it, or when you change states. Students admitted through central counselling processes in another state almost always need one.

The character or conduct certificate, signed by the head of your last institution, is another standard requirement. It simply confirms that your conduct was satisfactory during your time there.

## Identity and address proof

Most colleges ask for a government-issued photo ID. Aadhaar is the most common choice, and voter ID, passport, or driving licence also work. Make sure the name on your ID matches the name on your marksheets. Small spelling differences are one of the most common reasons students are sent back from the verification desk.

Aadhaar also works as address proof. If you are applying for a hostel, the warden's office may ask for a separate address document such as a ration card, an electricity bill, or a rent agreement.

## Category, income, and domicile certificates

If you are claiming a seat under a reserved category, carry your caste certificate, such as SC, ST, or OBC-NCL. It must be issued by a competent authority and should be in the format your college or counselling body specifies. OBC candidates usually need a non-creamy layer certificate that is recent, so check the validity period in the admission notice.

Students applying under the EWS quota need an EWS certificate. If you are applying for a scholarship or a fee concession along with admission, most colleges also ask for an income certificate. A domicile certificate is commonly required by state government colleges that reserve seats for residents of the state.

## Photographs and the application paperwork

Keep four to six recent passport-size photographs ready. Some colleges ask for photos with a light background, so it is worth checking the size and background specified in the notice.

Bring a printed copy of your submitted application form or your application number, along with the fee payment receipt. Students admitted through entrance exams should carry the admit card and scorecard. For postgraduate admissions, graduation marksheets and the degree certificate or provisional certificate are needed instead of Class 12 papers.

## Quota and special-category certificates

If you are seeking admission under a special quota, bring the relevant certificates in original. Sports quota applicants need certificates of participation or medals at the district, state, or national level, and NCC or NSS candidates should carry their certificates for weightage. Students with disabilities need a disability certificate issued by the competent medical authority. Some colleges also give weightage for certificates in co-curricular activities, so carry whatever the admission notice lists.

## A few practical tips before you leave home

Arrange your documents in the order the college lists them, and keep one extra set of photocopies in a separate folder. Verify every name and date of birth across documents before the admission day. If there is a mismatch, get it corrected through the board or issuing authority as early as possible, because corrections take time.

Students with a gap year after their last examination may be asked for a gap affidavit. If you fall in that category, prepare it in advance in the format your college prescribes. When everything is organised, the verification counter takes ten minutes. When it is not, it takes the whole day.
