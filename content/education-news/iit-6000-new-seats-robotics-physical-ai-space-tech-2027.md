IITs are set to add around 6,000 seats in robotics, physical AI and space engineering and technology, with the first batches starting in 2027. The Bhupat and Jyoti Mehta Family Foundation <a href="https://indianexpress.com/article/education/mehta-family-foundation-iits-6000-seats-robotics-physical-ai-space-technology-2027-jee-main-advanced-10976380/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">announced the plan on November 24</a>, in partnership with multiple IITs. The seats will be added in phases over eight to ten years, so all 6,000 will not be open in 2027 itself.

**Of the 6,000 seats, 5,000 are planned for robotics and physical AI and 1,000 for space engineering and technology, spread across BTech, master's and PhD programmes.**

## How the 6,000 seats break down

| Programme | BTech | Master's | PhD | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Robotics and physical AI | 2,500 | 1,200 | 1,300 | 5,000 |
| Space engineering and technology | 500 | 300 | 200 | 1,000 |
| Total | 3,000 | 1,500 | 1,500 | 6,000 |

More than 120 faculty members will be hired to teach the new programmes, the foundation said.

## What students will actually study

The robotics and physical AI courses will mix mechanical and electrical engineering with software, machine learning, computer vision, sensing and control systems. The research areas named in the announcement include foundation models, digital twins, humanoid and legged robots, swarm robotics and soft robotics.

Industry-linked capstone projects are part of the plan, and one participating IIT will offer a paid industry-residency semester. The teaching is meant to fit Indian working conditions, including heat, dust, power supply and cost limits. The uses named in the announcement include farm automation, rural healthcare and robots for small factories. Students will also study what automation does to jobs and inequality.

A School of Space Science and Technology is planned at one of the participating IITs. It will focus on satellite navigation, space robotics and human spaceflight. The foundation has not named the IIT that will host the school.

## Why the push is coming now

The numbers behind the plan are easy to see. The International Federation of Robotics counted 5.4 lakh industrial robots installed around the world in 2024. India installed 9,100 of them, its highest count so far, which put the country sixth in the world. India is also targeting a space economy of $44 billion by 2033. The foundation's own projection puts the global physical AI market at up to $1.4 trillion by 2035.

<a href="https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/mumbai/foundation-partners-with-iits-to-launch-robotics-physical-ai-and-space-engineering-programmes-by-2027/articleshow/136139524.cms" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Rahul Mehta</a>, who heads the foundation, put it this way: "A country's technological ambitions are usually measured in machines: the robots on its factory floors, the satellites in its skies. But behind every machine is a quieter question: who will build it? For India, the answer will depend partly on what happens in its classrooms and laboratories over the next decade."

The foundation has worked with IITs since 2019. It has helped set up eight schools and centres in data science and artificial intelligence, healthcare and biomedical engineering, and sustainability across six IITs. Those programmes have admitted more than 4,000 students so far. The new plan extends that work into robotics, physical AI and space technology.

## What is still not announced

Four things are missing from the announcement so far. The participating IITs have not been named. The way in is not clear yet, and nothing has been said about whether admission will run through JEE Advanced, GATE or a separate route. Fees and eligibility rules are not out. And the exact number of seats in the first batch in 2027 is not known either.

That means 2027 is the year the first batches start, not the year all 6,000 seats open. Students should treat the numbers above as the plan for the full build-out, not as the 2027 intake.

## What JEE aspirants should do now

For now, nothing in JEE preparation changes. [JEE Advanced 2027](/admissions/jee-advanced-2027-exam-date-may-23-iit-delhi-eligibility/) is scheduled for May 23, 2027, and it remains the main route into IIT BTech programmes. Students appearing for [JEE Main 2027](/admissions/jee-main-2027-session-1-registration-closes-nov-22-2026/) should keep their focus on the exam itself. The new programmes will appear in IIT admission notices when the institutes are ready to announce them, so the notices are the place to check in 2027.
