**NEET UG, the single biggest [medical entrance examination](/education-news/) in India, could one day be split into two stages.** A parliamentary panel has asked the National Testing Agency to study a format with a common prelims test for all medical streams and separate mains examinations for courses like MBBS, BDS, nursing and AYUSH. The goal is to stop nursing and AYUSH aspirants from being judged on the same single test as MBBS aspirants, a clash the panel calls avoidable competition.

The recommendation comes from the Parliamentary Committee on Education, chaired by Mukul Wasnik, in its report "Reforms and Strengthening of the National Testing Agency (NTA)", presented on Friday. The panel said trust in NTA's examinations is a matter of social justice and national integrity.

Before you worry, read this line twice: nothing has changed yet. The panel has asked the NTA to examine whether the two-stage format is feasible. It is a proposal, not an announcement.

## Common prelims, separate mains: what the panel wants

Right now NEET UG is a single-stage test that more than 22 lakh students take together. The panel compared this with the Civil Services Examination, which filters candidates through a multi-stage process.

Every medical aspirant would first write a common prelims test, then a mains examination for the course they actually want. A nursing aspirant would be tested and ranked separately from an MBBS aspirant.

> The Committee is of the view that NTA should examine the feasibility of a two-stage format for its large examinations, so that a candidate seeking nursing or AYUSH is tested and ranked apart from one seeking admission to MBBS, reducing avoidable competition.

## Age and attempt limits are back on the table

Age and attempt limits came up too. Today, NEET UG has no attempt cap and no effective upper age limit. The panel pointed to cases like candidates above the age of 70 appearing for the test.

The committee asked the Education Ministry, along with the Health Ministry and other stakeholders, to explore "reasonable" age and attempt limits for major examinations, including NEET UG. The one exception it named is UGC-NET, for which the usual rules would continue. Readers tracking [UGC NET December 2026 registration](/education-news/ugc-net-december-2026-registration-deadline-oct-28/) can stay with the existing pattern there.

No limit has been notified. These are recommendations for the ministries to study.

## Question banks, blacklisted vendors, and the late-entry rule

On paper leaks, the panel said the best long-term defence is a large, diverse, continuously updated question bank. It warned against both extremes: reusing the same question sets, and setting fresh papers every year with no bank behind them.

Three smaller but practical proposals stand out:

- A National Registry of blacklisted examination vendors, so agencies that fail on security cannot simply get hired again.
- Students who arrive late at the centre should be allowed to sit for the test after recording an undertaking, not turned away outright.
- Annual audits of examination results by state, gender, school board, medium of instruction and social category, to be published in the NTA's annual report.

On accountability, the panel wants the NTA answerable to Parliament, reporting yearly on cases under the Public Examinations Act.

For NEET UG itself, the panel backs a phased shift from pen and paper to computer-based testing, but only after real investment in government-owned test centres: 1,000 secure centres by 2030, at least one in every district.

The NTA's revenue surplus, currently parked in fixed deposits, could pay for these centres and for free mock tests.

Coaching institutes are in the frame too: the panel wants a regulatory framework covering fee transparency, safety and mental health, and says NEET, JEE Main and CUET should test the school curriculum, not coaching tricks.

## What this means for you right now

If you are preparing for NEET UG, nothing in your plan changes this week. The exam is still single-stage, and counselling still follows the MCC schedule. Readers following the current cycle can check the latest [NEET UG 2026 counselling updates](/education-news/neet-ug-2026-stray-vacancy-round-dates-eligibility/) for round-wise dates.

The conversation has moved, though. A parliamentary panel has now put two-stage NEET, age and attempt limits, and a CBT shift on the official agenda. If the NTA accepts any of this, the notice will come on the <a href="https://www.nta.ac.in" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official NTA website</a>. Treat every claim on social media about an "exam pattern change" as false until you see it there.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is NEET UG now a two-stage examination?**

No. The parliamentary panel has only asked the NTA to examine whether a two-stage format is feasible. The exam is unchanged and the NTA has announced nothing.

**Will there be an age limit or attempt limit for NEET UG?**

Not yet. The panel has asked both ministries to explore reasonable age and attempt limits. No limits have been notified.

**When could these changes take effect?**

No timeline. These are committee recommendations; the NTA and the government will decide what to accept and when.

**Where can I check official updates on NEET UG?**

Check the <a href="https://www.nta.ac.in" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official NTA website</a> for notices and schedules. Ignore forwarded messages and social media claims about exam changes.
