**Most Indian students love AI for speed, but they do not trust it for real learning. A new global survey says 82% of Indian students still reach for a pen and notebook when they want to remember what they studied.**

The finding comes from the HP Futures 2026 report, released in the UK this week. HP ran the study with the Global Learning Council and the Education Leaders Forum, and it covered more than 6,000 students across 37 countries. 758 of them were from India. 58 education, policy, and future-of-work leaders also shared their views in the report.

The numbers paint an interesting picture. Students are not rejecting AI. Far from it. They use it every day to finish assignments faster. But when the goal is to actually understand and remember a subject, they go back to old methods. According to the survey, 42% of Indian students prefer printed materials or handwritten notes for better retention. Only 17% picked AI-generated summaries.

## AI is fast, but the pen still wins for memory

This gap between speed and depth is the heart of the report. Students say AI helps them complete tasks quickly, yet they worry that it stops them from learning material in depth. Writing by hand forces the brain to process information slowly, and students clearly feel that difference.

Mayank Dhingra, Director and Global Head of Education Business and Strategy at HP, put it bluntly. "Most strikingly, Indian students warned that while they feel AI-confident and AI is helping them to work faster, they are concerned that they are not learning material as deeply," he told PTI. He added that student voices must be central as education systems decide the future of curricula and assessment.

The message is clear. Students are not asking for less AI in classrooms. They are asking for a smarter mix, where technology does not quietly replace the thinking part of learning.

## Despite the worry, students stay hopeful about jobs

Here is the surprise. The same students who worry about shallow learning are strongly positive about their careers. About 78% of Indian students believe AI will create more job opportunities than it will remove. 87% say they are confident they can use AI tools effectively and responsibly in their future careers.

Faith in the education system is high too. 92% of Indian students said their education is preparing them for the workplace. The overall mood is simple: AI is a powerful tool for work, but a risky shortcut for learning. Students seem to have figured out this difference on their own.

> "Education systems must do more to help students develop, demonstrate and continuously strengthen the capabilities that will remain valuable regardless of how technology evolves."
>
> — David McQuarrie, Chief Commercial Officer at HP and Chair of HP Futures

## Creativity and critical thinking top the skills list

When students were asked which skills will matter most in the future job market, AI and digital literacy came first at 52%. But the next three answers were all human skills: creativity at 48%, critical thinking at 41%, and communication and collaboration at 36%.

This matches what colleges and employers keep saying. AI can handle routine work, but employers still pay for people who can think, question, and create. For students planning careers, technical skills open doors, but creativity and critical thinking decide how far you go.

## What this means for students preparing for exams

The survey carries a practical lesson for anyone studying right now. AI summaries are fine for a quick overview, but the students surveyed say real retention still comes from writing things down yourself. Use AI to plan your study or to simplify a tough topic, then close the screen and write the key points in your own words.
