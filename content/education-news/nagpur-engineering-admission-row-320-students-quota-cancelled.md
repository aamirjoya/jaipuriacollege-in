**The future of 320 engineering students from Nagpur hangs in the balance. Their admissions under the Hindi linguistic minority quota were cancelled over alleged document irregularities, and the students say they found out only after they had finished their first year.** Now they have travelled to Mumbai to ask for help, and the Maharashtra Navnirman Sena has promised to take their case to Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis.

The students and their parents say they did not know their documents were being used to claim minority-quota seats. They say brokers and counsellors handled the paperwork. The CET Cell says every candidate got a chance to present their case, and the Bombay High Court's Nagpur Bench has dismissed the students' writ petitions and upheld the Admission Regulating Authority's decision.

## What happened to the 320 students

The row covers six engineering colleges in Nagpur and admissions for the 2024-25 and 2025-26 academic years. The colleges named in the complaints are GH Raisoni College of Engineering, GH Raisoni College of Engineering and Management, Priyadarshini College of Engineering, Priyadarshini Bhagwati College of Engineering, JD College of Engineering, and SB Jain Institute of Technology, Management and Research.

Trouble started when the Directorate of Technical Education checked the students' documents. Officials found leaving certificates from the same school carrying different signatures of the same principal. In some cases, a student had two leaving certificates, one showing Marathi as the mother tongue and the other showing Hindi, which was used to claim a seat under the Hindi linguistic minority quota.

The Admission Regulating Authority held a hearing in June. Of the students called, 301 appeared, while 15 stayed away. The authority then gave the students a chance to meet the document requirements. When they could not, it refused to approve the admissions.

## Brokers, forged signatures and lakhs in fees

The students' version of events is stark. According to Durgesh Sakulkar, Nagpur district president of the Maharashtra Navnirman Vidyarthi Sena, around 44 brokers acted as middlemen. They collected documents and fees of Rs 1 lakh to Rs 3 lakh from parents. One family is reported to have paid as much as Rs 8 lakh.

One student said the family went to a broker after the Class 12 results in 2025, hoping to get a concession in management-quota fees. The student had scored 70 percentile in MHT-CET and paid Rs 25,000 to the intermediary. The student alleged that counsellors put a dash instead of Marathi in the mother tongue column and forged the signature on Proforma O, the form needed for minority-quota admission. The family got the cancellation letter on August 21, 2026, after the student had finished the first year and moved into the second, having paid Rs 80,000 in college fees over the two years.

Other students alleged their families had paid counsellors Rs 2.5 lakh and Rs 4 lakh, without knowing the documents would be altered.

> "The students should not suffer for something that was not their fault. I will arrange a meeting with Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis, who has special powers to intervene, to ensure their 1.5 academic years are not wasted, and their education and future are not affected."

## Students take their case to Mumbai

At least 100 students and parents travelled from Nagpur to Mumbai in two buses on Friday and met Amit Thackeray at his residence in Dadar. They asked for a special Government Resolution to protect their academic interests and allow eligible students to continue their studies. The students have also approached MNS president Raj Thackeray, and asked for an impartial inquiry into the alleged involvement of brokers, college managements and officials.

The CET Cell has taken a different view. Commissioner Dilip Sardesai said all candidates were allowed to present their cases, and the Bombay High Court's Nagpur Bench had dismissed the writ petitions and upheld the Admission Regulating Authority's decision. That leaves the students with only one real hope now: a government decision in their favour.

## What this means for other admission seekers

This case is a warning for every family going through admissions. Fake documents and middlemen can cost a student years of study, even when the student did not know what was happening. The National Medical Commission recently warned MBBS aspirants about fake colleges and forged approval letters, and the same lesson applies here: check everything yourself before you pay. [Read the NMC warning on fake medical colleges](/education-news/nmc-warning-fake-medical-colleges-mbbs-admission-2026/).

- Apply only through the official counselling process run by the state CET Cell. Check the official <a href="https://mahacet.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Maharashtra CET Cell portal</a> for notices, merit lists and admission rules.
- Never hand your original documents to an agent, broker or counsellor. Submit them yourself or through the college office.
- Keep a copy of every form you sign, including Proforma O or any quota claim form. Check the mother tongue and category entries before you sign.
- Ask the college to show its approval for the course and the quota under which your seat is offered, in writing, before you pay fees.
- If someone asks for extra money to "manage" a seat, walk away and report it to the CET Cell or the Admission Regulating Authority.

The students in Nagpur are now waiting to see whether the Chief Minister steps in. For now, their 1.5 academic years hang on a decision not yet made.
